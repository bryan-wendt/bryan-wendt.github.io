# Pin Tumbler Mechanisms

The pin tumbler lock is the most common mechanical locking mechanism in North America for residential deadbolts, commercial mortise cylinders, and padlock housings.

---

## Anatomy & Operational Principle

A standard pin tumbler cylinder consists of:

- **Plug (Core):** The inner rotating cylinder containing the keyway and pin chambers.
- **Hull (Bible):** The outer stationary housing containing the upper driver pin chambers and springs.
- **Key Pins:** Bottom pins that contact the key cuts. Vary in length according to key bitting.
- **Driver Pins:** Upper pins situated above key pins, driven downward by springs across the shear line.
- **Shear Line:** The gap between the plug and the outer housing where rotation occurs.

When the correct key is inserted, all pin interfaces align precisely along the shear line, permitting the plug to rotate.

---

## Security Pin Variations

To resist basic manipulation, manufacturers incorporate modified driver pin profiles:

- **Spool Pins:** Narrow center waist designed to catch on the hull under tension, inducing a noticeable *false set* and *counter-rotation* when picking.
- **Serrated Pins:** Micro-grooves along the pin body that produce false clicks and catch multiple times during picking.
- **Mushroom Pins:** Tapered head that catches the hull edge, common in older European and European-influenced designs.

---

## Applicable Bypasses & Manipulation

| Technique | Description | Difficulty |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single Pin Picking (SPP)** | Individual lifting and setting of each pin stack at the binding point. | Low to Moderate |
| **Raking / Kinetic Attacks** | Fast scrubbing action to set multiple standard pins simultaneously. | Low |
| **Bumping** | Specially cut 999 bump key transferred kinetic energy to driver pins. | Low |
| **Comb Picking** | Over-lifting entire pin stacks into the bible (effective if plug chambers lack height stops). | Low |

## Defensive Countermeasures

- Utilizing high-security cylinders with sidebars and secondary locking elements (e.g., Medeco, ASSA Abloy).
- Retrofitting chambers with alternating spool and serrated security pins.
- Implementing patented, tightly paracentric keyways that restrict tension tool and pick accessibility.
