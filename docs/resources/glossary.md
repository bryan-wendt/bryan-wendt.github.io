# Physical Security & Locksmithing Glossary

Key terminology and technical concepts referenced throughout the wiki.

---

### A
- **Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK):** A low-frequency RFID modulation scheme that alters the amplitude of the carrier wave to represent binary data (e.g., EM4100).
- **Astragal:** A vertical molding or plate attached to the meeting edge of a door to cover the gap and protect latch hardware from external tampering.
- **Auxiliary Deadlatch Pin:** A secondary small pin alongside a latch bolt that, when depressed by the strike plate, prevents the main latch from being depressed or slipped.

### B
- **Bitting:** The specific pattern of depth cuts on a key blade designed to set pins or wafers to the correct heights.
- **Between Cut Centers (BCC):** The distance between consecutive cut positions along a key blade.
- **Bottom of Keyway (BOK):** Applying rotational tension by placing the wrench at the bottom (pin side) of the keyway.
- **Build-up Pin (Control Pin):** In SFIC systems, the specific pin placed in the chamber to bridge the difference between the operating cut and the control cut.

### C
- **Contact Area:** The angular span between the Left Contact Point and Right Contact Point on a safe lock drive cam.
- **Contact Point:** The specific rotational position on a safe dial where the lever nose makes metal-on-metal contact with the drive cam gate edges.
- **Control Key:** A dedicated key used in interchangeable core (SFIC/LFIC) systems to rotate the control sleeve and extract the core from its housing.
- **Control Shear Line:** The secondary upper shear plane in an SFIC cylinder that allows the control sleeve to turn independently of the plug.
- **Core (Plug):** The inner rotating portion of a lock cylinder that turns to activate the locking cam or tailpiece.
- **Crash Bar:** A horizontal egress device that unlatches a door when push pressure is applied.
- **Crypto-1:** The proprietary stream cipher designed by NXP Semiconductors for MIFARE Classic RFID smartcards.

### D
- **De Bruijn Sequence:** A cyclic sequence of characters from a given alphabet for which every possible sequence of length n appears as a substring exactly once; used to minimize keypad brute-force keystrokes.
- **Drive Cam:** The primary disc in a mechanical safe lock attached to the dial spindle that drives the wheel pack and features the drop-in gate.
- **Drop-In:** The mechanical event during safe lock dialing when all wheel gates align beneath the fence, allowing the lever nose to enter the drive cam gate.

### E
- **Effective Plug Diameter (EPD):** The outer diameter of a lock cylinder plug body.
- **ESPkey:** An open-source hardware implant installed inline on a Wiegand bus to passively sniff, log, and replay badge credentials.

### F
- **Facility Code (FC):** An 8-bit to 16-bit integer embedded within an access control credential bitstream designating the specific enterprise or installation site.
- **Fail-Safe:** An electric lock that unlocks automatically when electrical power is interrupted (e.g., standard magnetic locks).
- **Fail-Secure:** An electric lock that remains mechanically locked when electrical power is lost (e.g., standard electric strikes).
- **False Set:** A slight rotation of the plug that occurs when a spool or security pin catches on its narrow waist rather than fully setting.
- **Fence:** A rigid crossbar attached to the lever of a safe lock that must enter all wheel gates simultaneously to permit unlocking.
- **Frequency Shift Keying (FSK):** A modulation technique used in RFID systems (e.g., HID Prox) that toggles between two subcarrier frequencies to transmit data.

### G
- **Gate:** A rectangular notch milled into the outer circumference of a safe lock wheel disc.
- **Gate Signature:** A measurable shift or narrowing of the dial contact area indicating that a wheel gate has passed under the fence.

### I
- **iCLASS Elite:** A high-security configuration of HID iCLASS credentials utilizing a customized enterprise cryptographic key rather than standard factory keys.
- **Independent Service (IND):** An elevator operating mode that removes the car from the automated dispatch group, allowing exclusive manual control from within the cab.
- **Interchangeable Core (IC):** A modular cylinder engineered to be extracted and replaced in seconds using a control key without hardware disassembly.

### L
- **Left Contact Point (LCP):** The dial position where the lever nose contacts the left shoulder of the safe lock drive cam gate.
- **loclass:** An open-source cryptographic attack against HID iCLASS readers that recovers secret Elite Master Keys by simulating credentials to harvest reader authentication MACs.
- **Lost Motion:** Mechanical play engineered into safe lock drive flys that allows wheels to be parked while other wheels rotate in the opposite direction.

### M
- **Maximum Adjacent Cut Specification (MACS):** The maximum allowable numerical depth difference between adjacent cuts on a key blade.
- **Multiplex Keyway:** A hierarchical family of keyway wardings featuring change keys, multi-section sub-masters, and all-section grand master keys.

### O
- **1-Wire:** A low-speed single-data-conductor serial communications bus developed by Dallas Semiconductor for iButton fobs and sensors.
- **Operating Shear Line:** The primary shear boundary in an interchangeable core that allows the inner plug to turn and operate the lock throw.

### P
- **Paracentric:** A lock keyway design where warding projections extend past the vertical centerline of the key blade.
- **Phase 1 Emergency Recall:** An automated or key-triggered elevator life safety state that returns all cars non-stop to the ground floor.
- **Phase 2 Emergency In-Car Operation:** A key-controlled elevator mode allowing firefighters to manually navigate the car during emergencies.
- **Phase Shift Keying (PSK):** An RFID modulation scheme (e.g., Indala) that shifts the phase of the carrier waveform.

### R
- **Request to Exit (REX):** A sensor (PIR, motion, or switch) designed to detect an occupant approaching an exit and signal the electronic access system to unlock the door.
- **Right Contact Point (RCP):** The dial position where the lever nose contacts the right shoulder of the safe lock drive cam gate.

### S
- **Shear Line:** The circular boundary plane between the rotating plug and the stationary cylinder housing.
- **Single Pin Picking (SPP):** The method of lifting each pin individually to the shear line while feeling for binding order.
- **Small Format Interchangeable Core (SFIC):** A standardized figure-eight interchangeable core format based on the Best A-2/A-3/A-4 architecture.
- **Stack Constant:** The required total height of all components in an SFIC chamber stack (equal to 23 steps in the Best A-2 system).

### T
- **T5577:** An Atmel/Microchip multi-standard rewritable RFID transponder chip capable of emulating nearly any 125 kHz credential.
- **To First Cut (TFC):** The distance from the key stop reference point (shoulder or tip) to the center of the first pin cut.
- **Top of Keyway (TOK):** Placing a flat tension prybar at the top of the keyway to maximize pick mobility.

### W
- **Wheel Pack:** The assembly of coaxial combination discs and drive flys inside a mechanical safe lock.
- **Wiegand Protocol:** A standard 2-wire (Data 0 / Data 1) unidirectional communications interface connecting card readers to access control panels.
