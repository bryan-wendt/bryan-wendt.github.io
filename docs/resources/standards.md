# Physical Security & Lighting Standards

Architectural physical security relies on formal engineering standards that balance detection, physical delay, access control, and illumination. Assessing a commercial or critical infrastructure facility requires evaluating how its defenses measure against established standards from **ASIS International** and the **Illuminating Engineering Society (IES)**.

---

## The ASIS Defense-in-Depth Model

Under the **ASIS Facilities Physical Security Measures Standard**, protection is organized into four concentric layers of security:

```text
       [ Layer 1: Property Perimeter ] (Fences, Gates, Berms, Bollards)
                     |
                     v
       [ Layer 2: Building Exterior ] (Walls, Doors, Windows, Glazing)
                     |
                     v
       [ Layer 3: Building Interior ] (Lobbies, Corridors, Hallways)
                     |
                     v
       [ Layer 4: Secure Inner Areas ] (Server Rooms, Vaults, SCIFs)
```

### The Fundamental Delay-Response Equation
`Total Barrier Delay Time > Detection Time + Assessment Time + Response Force Transit Time`

If physical barriers delay an intruder for 3 minutes, but security response transit time takes 7 minutes, the perimeter defense has failed regardless of camera quality.

### Physical Barrier Classifications & Delay Times

| Barrier Type | Security Level | Representative Hardware | Penetration Delay Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Chain Link** | Low | 6-foot galvanized chain link, unreinforced | < 15 seconds (climbing or cable cutters) |
| **Industrial Anti-Climb**| Medium | 8-foot chain link with top 3-strand barbed wire | 1 to 2 minutes |
| **High-Security Weld-Mesh**| High | 358 Anti-Climb mesh (1/2" x 3" grid) with razor wire | 5 to 10 minutes |
| **Commercial Glass Door**| Low | 1/4" tempered architectural glass | < 10 seconds (impact hammer or punch) |
| **Forced Entry Glazing** | High | UL 972 / ASTM F1233 polycarbonate laminate | 5 to 20+ minutes against sledgehammers |

## IES Guidelines for Security Lighting (IES G-1-03)

Security lighting deters unauthorized entry, ensures clear human observation, and enables closed-circuit television (CCTV) optical sensor fidelity. The **Illuminating Engineering Society (IES)** defines standard illuminance guidelines in **foot-candles (fc)** and **lux (1 fc ≈ 10.76 lux)**:

| Facility Area / Zone | Minimum Maintained Illuminance | Uniformity Ratio (Max : Min) | Security Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Perimeter Fence Line** | 0.5 fc (5.4 lux) | 6 : 1 | Eliminate shadows along boundary fence line |
| **Property Buffer Zone** | 0.2 fc (2.2 lux) | 10 : 1 | General situational awareness |
| **Vehicular Gates & Checkpoints** | 5.0 fc (54 lux) | 3 : 1 | Rapid vehicle cab inspection, credential reading |
| **Pedestrian Access Doors**| 5.0 fc (54 lux) | 4 : 1 | Facial recognition, keyway and reader operation |
| **Building Exterior Perimeter** | 1.0 fc (10.8 lux) | 4 : 1 | CCTV motion detection and patrol verification |
| **High-Security Cash / Vault Areas**| 10.0 to 20.0 fc (108 to 215 lux) | 2 : 1 | Forensic detail, continuous high-resolution recording |

### Critical Lighting Design Criteria

1. **Vertical Illuminance:** While horizontal illuminance measures light falling on the ground, **vertical illuminance** (measured at 5 feet above the ground) determines whether facial features can be distinguished by witnesses and camera sensors.
2. **Glare Control:** Luminaires must be positioned and shielded to direct light away from security guards and CCTV cameras, projecting glare toward approaching intruders.
3. **Color Rendering Index (CRI):** Lighting should maintain a CRI of 70 or higher (preferably LED 4000K–5000K) to ensure accurate color rendering of intruder clothing, skin tone, and vehicle color on CCTV recordings.

## Authoritative Standards & Guidelines

- [ASIS International Standards & Guidelines (Physical Asset Protection Standard)](https://www.asisonline.org/publications--resources/standards--guidelines/)  
  Comprehensive standard governing physical protection systems, concentric layer design, and perimeter delay calculation.
- [IES G-1-16 / G-1-22: Guideline for Security Lighting for People, Property, and Critical Infrastructure](https://store.ies.org/product/g-1-16-guideline-for-security-lighting-for-people-property-and-critical-infrastructure/)  
  Official lighting engineering guideline defining photometrics, illuminance levels, uniformity ratios, and camera coordination.
- [Unified Facilities Criteria (UFC 4-010-01): DoD Minimum Antiterrorism Standards for Buildings](https://www.wbdg.org/ffc/dod/unified-facilities-criteria-ufc/ufc-4-010-01)  
  Federal engineering criteria establishing standoff distances, structural hardening, and glazing blast resistance standards.
