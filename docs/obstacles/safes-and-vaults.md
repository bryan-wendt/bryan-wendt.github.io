# Safes, Vaults & Security Containers

Physical security evaluations of sensitive data centers, financial institutions, and government facilities frequently encounter high-security safes, file cabinets, and modular vaults. Evaluating the defensive capability of a safe requires understanding the certified standards governing tool resistance, delay time, and structural construction.

Two primary rating systems dominate physical security:
1. **Underwriters Laboratories (UL 687):** The commercial standard for burglary-resistant safes.
2. **General Services Administration (GSA):** The US Federal standard for classified material security containers and vault doors.

---

## Underwriters Laboratories (UL 687) Burglary Ratings

UL 687 classifies safes by the **tools** an attacker can use and the **net working time** the container can withstand a direct physical attack against its door or body.

```text
       [ B-Rate / C-Rate ] (Unrated Commercial Steel Box)
               |
               v
        [ TL-15 / TL-30 ] (Tool Lathe: Hand tools, drills, grinders)
               |
               v
       [ TRTL-30 / TRTL-60 ] (Torch & Tool: Oxy-acetylene torches + power tools)
               |
               v
           [ TXTL-60 ] (Torch, Explosives & Tool: High explosives + all tools)
```

| UL Rating | Target Surface | Net Working Attack Time | Attack Tools Permitted in Certification Testing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **B-Rate** | Entire safe | No formal test | Non-rated industry standard; typically 1/8" to 1/4" body steel, 1/2" door |
| **C-Rate** | Entire safe | No formal test | Non-rated industry standard; typically 1/2" steel body, 1" solid steel door |
| **TL-15** | Door & Face | **15 Minutes** | Hand tools, sledgehammers, high-speed carbide drills, pressure rigs, abrasive wheels |
| **TL-30** | Door & Face | **30 Minutes** | Hand tools, carbide drills, diamond core drills, angle grinders, disc cutters |
| **TL-30x6** | **All 6 Sides** | **30 Minutes** | Same as TL-30, but tested across the door, back, top, bottom, and side walls |
| **TRTL-30** | Door & Face | **30 Minutes** | Power tools **plus** oxy-acetylene cutting torches and thermal cutting tools |
| **TRTL-60x6**| **All 6 Sides** | **60 Minutes** | Power tools, diamond equipment, core drills, and high-temperature cutting torches |
| **TXTL-60** | **All 6 Sides** | **60 Minutes** | Power tools, thermal torches, and **up to 8 oz nitroglycerin/explosive charges** |

### Understanding "Net Working Time"
UL test time is recorded using a stopwatch that **stops whenever the attack team is not actively touching the safe** (e.g., stopping to change drill bits, cool a cutting wheel, inspect a hole with a scope, or adjust a pressure rig). Therefore, a safe certified for a 15-minute net working time frequently resists several hours of real-world physical attack.

### The "x6" Designation
Standard TL-15 and TL-30 safes only guarantee certified protection on the **front door and faceplate within 2 inches of the door frame**. The side, top, and rear walls are often composed of significantly thinner steel filled with insulating drywall or aggregate. Safes bearing the `x6` suffix (e.g., TL-30x6) feature identical composite hardplate and re-enforced concrete fill across all six surfaces.

## Federal GSA Security Container Ratings

The US Federal Government classifies containers and vault doors under strict Federal Specifications (AA-F-357, AA-F-358, AA-F-2815). These ratings govern the storage of classified national security information (Collateral, Secret, Top Secret, and Special Access Programs):

| GSA Class | Certified Lock Types | Forced Entry Delay | Surreptitious Entry Delay | Covert Entry Delay | Primary Applications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Class 5** | FF-L-2740 (X-09/X-10) or Group 1R | **10 minutes** (hand tools); **20 minutes** (drilling) | **30 person-minutes** | **20 person-hours** | Weapons, ammunition, high-value crypto, Secret/Top Secret documents |
| **Class 6** | FF-L-2740 (X-09/X-10) | None (Protection against surreptitious entry only) | **30 person-minutes** | **20 person-hours** | Classified document files, map containers, electronic media |
| **Class 7** | S&G 8400 / Group 1R | None (Obsolescent standard) | **30 person-minutes** | **20 person-hours** | Historical government file containers (no longer manufactured) |
| **Class 8** | High-security vault | Heavy tool resistance | **30 person-minutes** | **20 person-hours** | Secure modular vault panels and vault doors |

### Entry Classification Definitions (GSA Standard)
- **Forced Entry:** Attack using heavy destructive tools (sledgehammers, carbide drills, angle grinders, oxy-acetylene torches). Leaves unmistakable, visually obvious destruction.
- **Surreptitious Entry:** Manipulating, decoding, or bypassing the locking mechanism or container without leaving physical evidence that is readily detectable by a casual user during normal operation.
- **Covert Entry:** Technical physical penetration that leaves no evidence detectable during standard physical inspection, requiring microscopic, forensic, or laboratory analysis to discover.

## GSA Container Physical Identification in the Field

When assessing GSA containers in federal or defense contractor facilities, verify container legitimacy and history through exterior data plates:

1. **GSA Approval Tag:** An aluminum label riveted to the exterior front face or drawer face.
   - **Color Coding:** Black lettering on silver aluminum indicates factory-manufactured original containers. Red lettering indicates refurbished, repaired, or recertified containers that have undergone factory welding or recoring under federal oversight.
2. **Cabinet Body Styles:**
   - **Mosler Safe Company:** Prominent round-cornered drawer edges, cast aluminum recessed drawer pulls, and internal drawer interlocking deadbars.
   - **Diebold Incorporated:** Beveled square-edge drawer fronts with integrated thumb latches.
   - **Schwab / Hamilton:** Modern square-profile welded multi-drawer cabinets and modular security files.
